The ARPANET

>> What Was It?


  • ARPANET stands for Advanced Research Projects Agency Network. Quite the mouthful, huh? But don't worry; it's more exciting than it sounds.
  • Picture this: It's the late 1960s, and the United States Department of Defense (specifically, the Advanced Research Projects Agency, or ARPA) is pondering how to connect computers across different locations. They wanted a network that could survive a nuclear attack (because, you know, Cold War vibes).
  • ARPANET wasn't just about connecting computers—it was about creating a new way for humans to communicate, collaborate, and share knowledge, even in the most challenging circumstances.

The ARPANET connected roughly 200 nodes.

Today, the internet consists of more than a billion connected devices, spanning the entire globe.

1960

What became of it?

Fast forward a few decades, and ARPANET's legacy lives on in the form of the modern internet, a vast and complex network that connects billions of people worldwide.

The Birth of Networking

In the 1960s, the concept of connecting computers over distances was revolutionary. ARPANET, the precursor to the modern internet, was the first step in creating a world where information could flow freely between distant locations, laying the groundwork for the global network we know today.
