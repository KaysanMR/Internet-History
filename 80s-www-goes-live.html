The 1980s: Laying the Foundation

>> Key Developments


  • TCP/IP Adoption (1983): This standardized protocol became the backbone of the modern internet.
  • Personal Computing Boom: Companies like Apple and IBM made computers accessible, fueling early internet growth.
  • World Wide Web (1989): Tim Berners-Lee proposed a system of hyperlinked documents, paving the way for the internet boom.

By the end of the 1980s, over 100k computers were connected to the internet, marking a huge leap from just a few hundred at the start of the decade.

Key Milestones of the 1980s

  • 1981: The Domain Name System (DNS) was introduced, simplifying internet navigation by allowing websites to have human-readable addresses.
  • 1983: ARPANET officially switched to the TCP/IP protocol, standardizing internet communication and enabling the network to expand globally.
  • 1989: Tim Berners-Lee proposed the World Wide Web, a system that would make information on the internet more accessible and user-friendly.

1980s Internet Culture

The 1980s marked the beginning of what would become modern internet culture. Although still primarily used by researchers and academics, the concept of global networking started to take hold. This era also saw the first inklings of the online communities and digital communication that would later flourish in the 1990s and beyond.
