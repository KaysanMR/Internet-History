The term “internet” is born.


  • In the early 1970s, researchers began to see the need for a universal way to describe the interconnection of multiple networks. This led to the coining of the term "internet."
  • The term was derived from the concept of "internetworking," which described the process of connecting different networks to work together as a single cohesive system.
  • This was a critical development in the history of technology, as it marked the beginning of the global digital communication system we know today.
  • By the mid-1970s, the internet as a concept began to take shape, laying the groundwork for the rapid technological advances that would follow in the coming decades.

1970

The Evolution of Network Protocols

In the 1970s, several key protocols were developed to support the growing network of computers. These protocols, like TCP/IP, are still in use today, forming the backbone of the internet.


The Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) and the Internet Protocol (IP) were critical in allowing computers to communicate across different networks, making it possible to exchange data reliably over long distances.

By the late 1970s, the number of devices connected to the emerging internet was growing rapidly. What began with just a few computers was expanding into a worldwide network.

The 1970s laid the foundation for the internet as we know it. Innovations in network protocols, combined with the coining of the term "internet," set the stage for the rapid expansion and development of digital communication technologies.
